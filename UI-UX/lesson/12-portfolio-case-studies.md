# Portfolio Case Studies

## Why this matters

A lead-level portfolio demonstrates judgment, not just polished screens. It explains how you navigated ambiguity, used evidence, influenced decisions, and delivered an outcome.

## Core concepts

- **Context:** The product, users, business problem, team, timeline, and constraints.
- **Your role:** Clearly distinguish your responsibility from the work of collaborators.
- **Process as evidence:** Show only the research, flows, prototypes, and iterations that explain a meaningful decision.
- **Tradeoffs:** Demonstrate what you chose not to do and why.
- **Impact:** Report outcomes honestly, including limitations and what you would improve next.

## Think through

1. What difficult problem did you solve, and why did it matter?
2. What did you personally lead, design, research, or influence?
3. Which evidence changed your direction?
4. How did technical, policy, time, or business constraints affect the solution?
5. What outcome was measured, and what remains uncertain?

## Practice

Turn the enterprise workflow project from this learning track into a case study. Use only artifacts that strengthen the story of your decisions and impact.

## Deliverable

Write a case study with sections for context, problem, role, research, strategy, exploration, final solution, collaboration, impact, and reflection.

## Common interview questions

### Walk me through a project you led from problem to outcome.

Use a concise story: establish the users, business context, and problem; explain your role and the evidence you gathered; describe the key decisions and tradeoffs; show how you partnered with product and engineering; and close with the outcome and what you learned. Focus on your judgment and influence, not a screen-by-screen tour.

### Tell me about a design that did not work as expected.

Choose an example where you can be candid and accountable. Explain the assumption, how evidence or results showed it was wrong, what you changed, and how you prevented the same issue from recurring. A strong answer shows learning, research discipline, and the ability to adapt rather than trying to present an error-free process.

## Key terms / cheat sheet

- **Case study:** A structured story showing your role, reasoning, work, and impact.
- **Constraint:** A limitation that shaped a decision, such as time, policy, or technical feasibility.
- **Impact:** A verified or observed result of the work.

## Scenario challenge

Turn an internal workflow redesign into a 10-minute interview presentation. Choose the evidence and artifacts that demonstrate strategic leadership instead of simply showing every screen.

## Common pitfalls

- Showing polished screens without explaining the problem or your role.
- Claiming team outcomes as individual work.
- Hiding unsuccessful iterations instead of explaining learning and adaptation.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Context, problem, role, constraints, and collaborators are clear.
- [ ] Artifacts support key decisions rather than documenting every activity.
- [ ] Impact is specific, honest, and connected to your contribution.

## Portfolio evidence

Use the case study itself: include a concise presentation version, a detailed written version, and supporting artifacts you can discuss in an interview.

## Flashcards

**Q:** What should a lead-level case study demonstrate?  
**A:** Strategic judgment, evidence-based decisions, cross-functional influence, execution quality, and impact.

**Q:** How should you discuss an unsuccessful design decision?  
**A:** Explain the assumption, evidence, adjustment, and learning with clear accountability.
