# Cross-Functional Collaboration and UX Leadership

## Why this matters

Lead designers create alignment across product, engineering, business, and design. They make decisions understandable, surface tradeoffs early, and help others do better work.

## Core concepts

- **Facilitation:** Structure conversations so participants can contribute, decide, and leave with ownership.
- **Design critique:** Give feedback against user goals, principles, evidence, and constraints rather than personal taste.
- **Stakeholder management:** Identify decision makers, affected partners, concerns, and communication needs.
- **Mentorship:** Help designers grow through clear expectations, context, feedback, and opportunities to practice.
- **Design operations:** Rituals, templates, reviews, and working agreements that improve quality and delivery.

## Think through

1. What decision needs alignment, and who owns it?
2. What evidence, prototype, or artifact will make the tradeoff concrete?
3. Which concerns are valid constraints versus untested assumptions?
4. How can feedback be specific, actionable, and connected to outcomes?
5. How can a designer take the next step in craft or strategic ownership?

## Practice

Plan a 60-minute workshop to align a team on a workflow problem. Include objectives, participants, agenda, activities, artifacts, and the decision expected at the end.

## Deliverable

Create a workshop plan and a design-critique template with goals, evidence, discussion prompts, decisions, owners, and follow-up actions.

## Common interview questions

### How do you handle disagreement with product or engineering?

I make sure we agree on the decision, constraints, and desired outcome before debating solutions. I listen for valid concerns about feasibility, scope, or business risk, then use prototypes, evidence, and explicit tradeoffs to compare options. If a decision remains unresolved, I identify the accountable owner and document the rationale so the team can move forward.

### How do you mentor less experienced designers?

I give context, set a clear level of ownership, and review work against user goals rather than just visual output. I ask questions that build their reasoning, give specific and timely feedback, and create opportunities for them to lead a portion of research, a critique, or a stakeholder conversation. I adapt the support to their growth area while maintaining quality standards.

## Key terms / cheat sheet

- **Facilitation:** Structuring a group discussion to reach a useful outcome.
- **Decision owner:** The person accountable for making the final call.
- **Design critique:** Feedback grounded in goals, evidence, and criteria.

## Scenario challenge

Product wants a faster release, engineering flags technical risk, and design research shows a critical workflow gap. Plan a 45-minute alignment session that reaches a documented decision.

## Common pitfalls

- Treating a design review as a presentation instead of a decision-making forum.
- Letting the loudest stakeholder set direction without evidence.
- Giving vague feedback that does not help a designer improve.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The meeting has a decision, owner, and clear participants.
- [ ] Evidence and tradeoffs are visible before discussion begins.
- [ ] Decisions, actions, and follow-ups are documented.

## Portfolio evidence

Show a workshop plan, critique framework, stakeholder map, and an example of how collaboration changed the outcome.

## Flashcards

**Q:** What makes feedback actionable?  
**A:** It identifies an observed issue, ties it to a goal or criterion, and suggests a next step.

**Q:** What is the purpose of facilitation?  
**A:** To help a group contribute, align, and make progress toward a shared decision.
