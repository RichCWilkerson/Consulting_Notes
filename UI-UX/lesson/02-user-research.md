# User Research and Insight Integration

## Why this matters

Research reduces the risk of building the wrong thing. A UX lead turns qualitative observations and quantitative behavior into decisions the product team can act on.

## Core concepts

- **Generative research:** Learn about users, needs, context, and unmet opportunities before defining a solution.
- **Evaluative research:** Test whether a proposed or existing experience works.
- **Qualitative evidence:** Interviews, observations, and usability sessions explain why behavior happens.
- **Quantitative evidence:** Analytics, surveys, funnels, and experiments show how often behavior happens.
- **Hypothesis:** A falsifiable belief that links a design change to an expected outcome.
- **Synthesis:** Grouping evidence into themes, insights, opportunities, and recommendations.

## Think through

1. What decision must this research inform?
2. Which user segment is most important to include?
3. What method can answer the question with the least effort and risk?
4. What evidence would disprove your current assumption?
5. How will an insight change a product or design decision?

## Practice

Draft a usability test for a five-step workflow. Define the participant profile, task scenario, success criteria, moderator questions, and the observations you will capture.

## Deliverable

Create a research plan with objectives, hypotheses, method, participants, timeline, analysis approach, and decision owners.

## Common interview questions

### How do you decide which research method to use?

I start with the decision the team needs to make. Interviews and observation help uncover needs and context; usability testing evaluates whether people can use a design; analytics identifies behavioral patterns at scale. I choose the lightest method that can produce credible evidence within the available time, then state what the method cannot tell us.

### How do you turn research findings into action?

I separate observations from insights and recommendations. I group evidence into themes, connect each theme to a user or business impact, and prioritize opportunities by severity, reach, and feasibility. I share concrete artifacts such as clips, task failures, or journey maps so the team understands the evidence, then record the decision and what we will measure after release.

## Key terms / cheat sheet

- **Research objective:** The decision-oriented question a study must answer.
- **Insight:** An evidence-based interpretation that explains a meaningful pattern.
- **Triangulation:** Comparing multiple sources or methods to increase confidence.

## Scenario challenge

A product team believes a new dashboard will reduce support calls. Create a study that tests both whether users understand it and whether the expected behavior changes.

## Common pitfalls

- Starting research without a decision the findings will inform.
- Treating participant quotes as representative without looking for patterns.
- Reporting findings without a clear recommendation or owner.

## Review checklist

- [ ] Objectives, hypotheses, and participants are appropriate for the decision.
- [ ] Tasks avoid leading participants toward the desired answer.
- [ ] Findings distinguish evidence, insight, and recommendation.

## Portfolio evidence

Show the research question, method rationale, synthesis artifact, recommendation, and the resulting product decision.

## Flashcards

**Q:** When is usability testing most useful?  
**A:** When evaluating whether representative users can understand and complete a task with a design.

**Q:** What makes a research insight actionable?  
**A:** It links evidence to a user or business impact and suggests a decision or opportunity.
